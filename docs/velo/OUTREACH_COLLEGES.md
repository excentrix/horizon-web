# VELO — College Outreach Playbook (Bangalore)

> Cold-call script + pilot motion for placement cells and coding clubs. Pairs with
> [DISTRIBUTION.md](./DISTRIBUTION.md) (the funnel) and [VOICE.md](./VOICE.md) (tone).
> **Goal of a call:** not a sale — a **free pilot with a batch of final-years** + the TPO's read on
> whether it's useful. North-star: completed verifications + recruiter interest in the cohort.

## The one principle
You are **Sid, a builder in Bangalore**, not a company doing outreach. You don't pitch a product — you
**lead with a true insight about their students** and ask if they're seeing it too. Then you let them talk.
It's a conversation about their reality, not a demo.

---

## Step 0 — Get the insight FIRST (do this before any call)
The opener only works if it's **true**. VELO is a truth product; don't fake research. Fortunately this is
half a day of work and it makes every call bulletproof.

**How to gather it (an afternoon):**
- Talk to **15–25 final-year students** (a WhatsApp group, one club, a campus visit, DMs). Ask 3 questions:
  1. "Could you walk a hiring manager through a project on your resume and defend every decision?"
  2. "How much of it did you build with AI/tutorial help — and could you rebuild or explain that part alone?"
  3. "How confident are you about the technical interview where they go deep on your projects?"
- Tally it. You now have a real number.

**What you'll almost certainly find (confirm, don't assume):**
- *Most weren't sure they could defend their own resume projects* — especially the AI-assisted parts.
- *Many can't cleanly separate "what I built" from "what the tutorial/AI built."*
- *The project deep-dive is their #1 interview fear.*

Write your actual finding here once you have it:
> **Our finding (as of ___):** We spoke to **[N]** final-year students across **[campuses]**. **[X of N]**
> weren't confident they could defend their own resume projects under real questioning. Fill this in — real.

## The call — live flow (what they say → what you say)

> Read top to bottom. **YOU** = your line. **THEM** = likely responses. ✅ = they're leaning in ·
> ❌ = pushback · ↩︎ = neutral/defer. Legend for the whole call: after your hook, **stay silent** — the
> first person to talk loses the point. Never argue a "no."

### ① Opening — get permission (≈10 sec)
**YOU:** "Hi [Name], I'm Sid — a software engineer here in Bangalore. I'll be quick, and I'm honestly not
selling anything. Is now an okay two minutes?"

- ✅ **"Sure / go ahead"** → go to ②.
- ↩︎ **"Who is this? / What's this about?"**
  **YOU:** "Sid — a developer in Bangalore. I run a free thing for final-year students before placements
  and I wanted a placement cell's honest take. Ninety seconds — can I?" → go to ②.
- ❌ **"Not a good time / I'm busy."**
  **YOU:** "Totally fair. Better later today or tomorrow morning? I'll call back then." → get a slot, exit warm.

### ② The hook — lead with the insight, then stop talking
**YOU:** "Last week we sat with **[N]** final-year students across a few Bangalore campuses and asked one
thing: *could you walk a hiring manager through a project on your resume and defend every decision?*
**[X of N]** weren't sure they could — especially the parts they built with AI. **Is your department
feeling that too?**"

- ✅ **"Yes, we see this."** *(also: "It's a real problem." / "Interviews expose it.")*
  **YOU:** "Right — it's the pattern on every campus. AI made it easy to ship a project you can't explain,
  and recruiters have stopped trusting 'I built X.' So the students who did the real work get buried with
  the ones who didn't. That's the one thing I built a tool for." → go to ③.
- ❌ **"No — our students are strong / we prep them."**
  **YOU:** "Honestly, that's rare — good on your program. Then this is even simpler: what I built lets a
  student *prove* it on the spot — an honest interview about their own project. If they're as strong as you
  say, it's a clean way to show recruiters 'our final-years are verified, not just claiming.' Worth a free
  batch to see them stand up?" → go to ③ (framed as a **showcase**, not a fix).
- ↩︎ **"Not sure / we haven't measured it."**
  **YOU:** "That's the real gap — nobody measures it, so nobody can fix it or prove it. What I built
  measures it, student by student, free for the first batch. Want to know your actual numbers before
  placement season?" → go to ③ (framed as a **free diagnostic**).

### ③ The offer + the ask
**YOU:** "The offer is simple: free, for **20–30 of your final-years** this month. Each picks a project
they actually built; the tool interviews them on their own work and gives an honest read on how well they
can defend it. They get a reality check; you get a summary — which skills hold up, where the gaps are,
which students to put in front of recruiters. I mostly want your feedback on whether it's useful. **Could
we run one batch?**"

- ✅ **"Okay / sounds interesting / yes, how does it work?"** → go to ④A (lock it).
- ❌ **An objection** ("is it really free?", "we already use HackerRank", "send an email", "how do I know
  it's good?", "need approval") → answer from the **Objections cheat-sheet** below, then **re-ask**:
  *"So — worth trying one free batch?"* → ✅ go to ④A · defer go to ④B.
- ❌ **"We're not interested."** → go to ④C.

### ④ Close (pick the track)

**④A — They're in (lock the pilot):**
**YOU:** "Great. Two ways — I come to campus and run the first batch with you in the room, or we do it over
a shared link. Which is easier? And which batch — a department, or your placement shortlist?"
→ Nail down: **date · batch size · your contact.** "I'll send a one-line confirmation now." Exit:
"Thanks [Name] — I'll make your students look good."

**④B — Soft yes ("send an email" / "need HOD approval"):**
**YOU:** "Will do — one paragraph, nothing to wade through. Can I call you **[day]** once you've seen it?
And who signs off — can I use your name when I reach out?"
→ Get: **email · a callback day · the approver's name.** Send the email within the hour. Never leave
without a next action *and* a date.

**④C — A no (exit clean, keep the door open):**
**YOU:** "No worries — thanks for the straight answer. Two quick things: mind if I check back before
placement season in case it's useful then? And is there a coding-club or faculty lead you'd point me to who
might want to run it with students directly?"
→ Get a referral if you can. Leave warm — Bangalore placement cells talk to each other.

> you if you can actually defend it — proof-of-work for the AI era. I'm giving it to a few coding clubs
> free before placements, with a little 'who's verified' leaderboard. Want to run it with your members?
> Costs nothing and makes the club look useful right before placement season."

## Objections
- **"Is it free? What's the catch?"** — "Genuinely free for the pilot. I need real students using it more
  than I need money right now. If it's useful later we can talk — no obligation."
- **"We already use HackerRank / coding tests."** — "Those test if they can solve a puzzle. This tests
  whether they understand the thing they actually built and put on their resume — the exact thing a hiring
  manager probes. Different signal. Worth seeing side by side."
- **"Send an email."** — "Will do, one paragraph. Can I follow up next week once you've seen it? Any batch
  or department you'd want to start with?" *(always attach a next action.)*
- **"How do I know it's any good?"** — "You don't yet — fair. That's why the first batch is free and you
  see the results yourself. If the read on your students doesn't match reality, you've lost nothing."
- **"Need HOD / principal approval."** — "Happy to send something you can forward — a free skills pilot for
  final-years. Who makes that call, and can I use your name when I reach out?"

## After a "yes" — the pilot (what you're actually setting up)
A **college cohort pilot** (from DISTRIBUTION.md):
- **20–40 final-year students** verify ≥1 real project; a **leaderboard** of who's verified goes live.
- The cell gets a **cohort report** ([`assets/sample-cohort-report.html`](./assets/sample-cohort-report.html)
  — sample): readiness distribution, cohort dimension averages, the **top knowledge gaps to train**, and a
  **showcase shortlist** of placement-ready students to send recruiters. This is the deal-closer — the
  artifact that turns a free student pilot into a paid college engagement.
- **Success = cohort verified + recruiter interest.** That's your case study for the next 10 colleges.
- Run the first batch **in person** if you can — you'll learn more and they trust it more.

**Honesty guardrails (don't torch credibility):**
- It's **early** — say so. You're inviting students to test it, not selling a finished product.
- Promise an **honest read** on where students stand — **never** promise placements or jobs.
- Don't overclaim the tech. It interviews a student on their own project and scores defensibility; it's not
  magic. Under-promise, let the results talk.
